## Minimal claim

```text
XCLAIM mystream mygroup worker-b 60000 1710000000000-0
```

- The fourth argument is **min-idle-time (milliseconds)**: only entries in the PEL with idle ≥ that value are transferred to `worker-b`.  
- It returns the claimed entries (with payload by default). You still need to **XACK** after processing, or the entry just lands in the new owner's PEL.

## Common modifiers

```text
XCLAIM mystream mygroup worker-b 60000 <id> JUSTID
XCLAIM mystream mygroup worker-b 0 <id> FORCE
XCLAIM mystream mygroup worker-b 60000 <id> IDLE 0 RETRYCOUNT 1
```

- **JUSTID**: change ownership only, don't return the body (saves bandwidth; use it when you already have a local copy).  
- **FORCE**: ignore idle and forcibly change the owner (use with care; suited to ops when you know for sure the original consumer is dead).  
- **IDLE / RETRYCOUNT**: reset the idle time and retry count after claiming, so someone else doesn't immediately grab it again based on the old idle.

## XAUTOCLAIM: scan the PEL instead of naming IDs

```text
XAUTOCLAIM mystream mygroup worker-b 60000 0-0 COUNT 20
```

Scans a batch of timed-out pending entries from a cursor; a good fit for a worker heartbeat loop where "if one dies, someone else picks it up". You still need idempotent processing + ACK.

## Double-consumption checklist

1. **Idempotency on a business key** (order ID / dedup table); a claim doesn't mean "nobody else has done it".  
2. Set min-idle above the P99 of "the slowest normal processing run", so live consumers are less likely to be robbed by mistake.  
3. When the original consumer recovers, it should check the PEL / its own claim state first, rather than blindly ACKing entries that have changed hands.  
4. Monitoring: pending count per consumer, idle percentiles, claim count.

## Dead letters

Streams has no DLQ out of the box. Common approaches: after more than N claims, `XADD dlq ...` and then ACK in the original group, or use a separate `XGROUP` on an isolation stream. Base the threshold on RETRYCOUNT or your own attrs.

## Summary

A claim is **ownership transfer + continued at-least-once delivery**, not magic deduplication. Put idle, FORCE, idempotency, and ACK into the same runbook, and the PEL stops being a black box.
